Ad rules

Indian ad rules in plain words

Meta or Google approving your ad doesn't mean it's legal in India. These are the rules that catch small businesses most often, in plain words.

By the AdSure ad team · Updated 24 November 2026 · 7 min read

Last checked: November 2026. This is a simple summary to help you avoid common mistakes, not legal advice. For a specific case, ask a lawyer.

1. Claims must be true and provable (ASCI)

The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) code says claims in ads must be truthful and backed by proof. In practice:

  • Avoid "guaranteed", "100%", "best", "No.1", "permanent" or "instant results" unless you can show proof. Use specific facts, like "rated 4.6 by 2,300 buyers".
  • A disclaimer can explain a claim, but it can't contradict it or hide important conditions. "Free*" with a big condition in small print is not OK.
  • If you say "free", say the condition next to it, for example "Free gift on orders above ₹999".

2. Influencer and creator ads need a clear label

  • If a creator got money, free products or anything else from you, the post must say so upfront with a label like "Ad", "Sponsored", "Collaboration", "Partnership" or "Free gift". Not hidden among hashtags.
  • In videos the label must stay on screen long enough to read. ASCI asks for at least 3 seconds on videos up to 15 seconds, and a third of the video for videos of 15 seconds to 2 minutes.
  • Creators giving health or nutrition advice should have the right qualifications, and stock market advice needs SEBI registration.
  • Under the Consumer Protection Act, the CCPA can fine misleading ads and can stop endorsers from endorsing for a period.

3. Health ads: never claim to cure a listed disease

The Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 bans ads that claim a product can cure, treat or prevent the diseases listed in it. The list includes diabetes, obesity, cancer, heart disease and blood pressure. It also covers claims about sexual capacity and menstrual problems. Penalties include jail.

  • Use careful words like "supports" or "helps manage", backed by evidence. Never use "cures" or "permanent results".
  • On Meta, health, weight loss and weight gain ads should only be shown to people aged 18 and over.

4. Loans and finance ads (RBI and platform checks)

  • Under RBI's Digital Lending Directions, loan offers must name the regulated lender, such as the bank or NBFC, and show the key facts, including the annual percentage rate (APR).
  • Don't present an RBI listing as RBI approval or endorsement, and don't use pressure tricks.
  • Google requires financial services advertisers in India to be verified before ads can run. Meta has its own checks for finance and stock advice.

5. Real-money gaming ads are banned

The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 bans offering and advertising online money games, like fantasy sports for cash, rummy or poker for money, and betting. It has been in force since 1 May 2026. Advertising can mean up to 2 years in jail and a fine of up to ₹50 lakh. Gaming companies have challenged the law in the Supreme Court, but the court has not paused it, so the ban applies. Google also stopped rummy and daily fantasy sports ads in India from January 2026.

6. No fake urgency or tricks

  • "Only 2 left!" or "Offer ends tonight" must be true. India's dark pattern guidelines (2023) and ASCI's rules on deceptive design ban false urgency and similar tricks.
  • Don't make ads look like news stories or use a news outlet's name or logo to sell.
  • Crypto and NFT ads need ASCI's risk disclaimer.
