Before you spend: a 12-point check for your ad

Most wasted ad money comes from a few common mistakes. Go through these 12 points before you put money behind an ad. It takes about 10 minutes.

By the AdSure ad team · Updated 24 November 2026 · 6 min read

The creative

  1. Show the product in the first second. On Reels and Stories most people decide in a moment whether to keep watching. If your product or brand only appears after a few seconds, many people never see it.
  2. Keep text on the image short. A few words for the offer is enough. A busy image hides the product and is harder to read on a phone.
  3. Use the right sizes. Square (1:1) or vertical (4:5) for feeds, and full-screen vertical (9:16) for Reels and Stories. A stretched or cropped creative looks careless.

The ad text

  1. One clear offer. Say what it is, what it costs or what someone gets, and why now. If a person can't repeat your offer after reading it once, it isn't clear yet.
  2. No claims you can't prove. Words like "guaranteed", "100%", "best" or "No.1" need proof you can show. They can get the ad rejected and break Indian ad rules. Use real numbers instead, like "4.6 stars from 2,300 reviews". See our guide to Indian ad rules.
  3. A button that matches the goal. "Shop now" for sales, "Sign up" or "Get quote" for leads, "Send message" for WhatsApp. A vague "Learn more" on a sales ad gets fewer buyers.

The landing page

  1. Same offer and price as the ad. If the ad says ₹899 and the page shows ₹1,199, people leave. The first thing they see after clicking should match what made them click.
  2. Fast on a phone. Open the page on your own phone using mobile data. Google treats a page whose main content shows within 2.5 seconds as "good". Heavy images and too many apps are the usual reasons a page is slow.
  3. Reasons to trust you. Reviews, a way to contact you (WhatsApp works well in India), a clear return or refund policy and a secure https link.

Tracking, budget and audience

  1. Tracking works before you spend. Make a test purchase or lead and check that Meta Events Manager or Google Ads records it. Without this, the platform can't learn who buys, and you can't tell which ad works.
  2. Enough budget for each ad set. Meta says an ad set needs about 50 results in 7 days to leave the learning phase. A simple rule: daily budget per ad set ≈ your target cost per result × 50 ÷ 7. If that's too much, run fewer ad sets, usually just one, before you cut anything else.
  3. Right audience and right time. Don't narrow the audience so much that it can't deliver. Exclude people who already bought. And plan around festivals and seasons: people start searching for festive products weeks before the day. See the 2027 festival ad calendar.
